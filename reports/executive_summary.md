## What matters

1. **The books do not close.** 1,220 orders (1.227%) fail the cash-to-revenue reconciliation, and R$ 162,591.95 of cash sits on orders for which the dataset holds no merchandise record at all.
2. **Refund exposure is R$ 269,735.11**, 1.68% of cash collected, and the monthly rate moves from 0.331% to 5.381% (16x) between the best and worst month.
3. **Instalments are the biggest modelled cost.** R$ 990,121.38 of modelled instalment funding on R$ 10,101,491.12 of instalment-funded card volume - larger than acquiring cost and promotional credit combined.
4. **Logistics is deteriorating as the business scales.** The freight ratio moved +1.08 points year on year while cash grew 136.98%.
5. **Dispute risk rises with instalment depth** - from 12.964% at one instalment to 21.081% at thirteen or more, covering R$ 2,769,252.32 of cash.
6. **Growth is acquisition, not retention.** Cash grew 136.98% like-for-like while average order value grew 0.81% and only 3.119% of customers ever reordered.

## What this analysis does not claim

- The source data has no refund, chargeback, complaint, logistics-cost or marketing-spend table. Refund exposure and disputed value are proxies; no intervention has been measured, so no saving or avoided loss is claimed.
- The cost-to-serve model uses published market rates. Every modelled number carries a sensitivity band and is labelled as modelled.
- The dataset is real, anonymised commercial data from 2016-2018. It is a historical extract; the business context is a marketplace, not a bank.
